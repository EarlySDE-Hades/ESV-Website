    Homework Before the Handshake

    Rounds stall in diligence, not on the pitch. The checklist to finish before your first investor meeting.

    Siddarth MenonInvestment Associate, Earlyseed Ventures

    In short

    Rounds rarely stall on the pitch. They stall weeks later, when an interested investor asks for documents and the answers take too long. Here is the homework to finish before the first meeting.

    Why the homework matters

    Most founders spend their preparation on the pitch. In Siddarth’s experience, that is rarely where a round stalls. It stalls a few weeks later, in diligence, when the paperwork does not match the story.

    Every gap costs less to fix before investors see the company. Once a fund is checking, each one becomes a delay, a negotiating point or a reason to pass.

    The checklist

    Three that catch founders out

    • The cap table. A co-founder who left two years ago still holds 15%. An angel was promised equity over WhatsApp that was never issued. An ESOP pool appears in the deck but the board never approved it.
    • Numbers that do not match. If the deck says ₹4 crore of revenue and the books say ₹3.2 crore, the investor stops asking about growth and starts asking what else they cannot trust.
    • Slow answers. Send your MIS on the day of the first call. A two-week wait loses the interest the call created.

    Two tips

    • Show the plan without the round. One slide comparing growth on current cash with growth on the new money shows the business can survive, and that the money speeds up a plan that already works.
    • Start early. A round usually takes six months from the first meeting to money in the bank. Check your timing with the runway calculator.
