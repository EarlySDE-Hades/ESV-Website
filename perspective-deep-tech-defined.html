    Deep Tech, Defined

    What counts as deep tech, how investors measure it on the TRL scale, and why the software playbook does not fit.

    Siddarth MenonInvestment Associate, Earlyseed Ventures

    In short

    Deep tech is now a legal category in India. What makes a company deep tech is the kind of risk it carries: the science might not work. Investors measure progress on a nine-step scale, and the usual software playbook does not fit.

    What counts as deep tech

    “Deep tech” gets used for everything from satellites to an app with a chatbot. Siddarth’s test is simple: what is the company’s biggest risk?

    For most startups, it is that customers will not pay enough. For a deep-tech company, it is that the technology will not work, or not at a cost anyone can live with. The science comes before the market.

    Since 4 February 2026, India’s rules agree. A DPIIT notification created a separate deep-tech category. A company has to meet four conditions together:

    • It works on new science or engineering that is still being developed.
    • It spends a high share of its money on R&D.
    • It owns, or is building, significant new intellectual property.
    • It faces long timelines, heavy capital needs and real technical uncertainty.

    Using AI or building hardware is not enough on its own. A tool built on someone else’s AI model is a software business, with software risks.

    How investors measure it

    Most deep-tech conversations use the Technology Readiness Level (TRL) scale, which NASA created in the 1970s. Its nine levels fall into three stages: lab, pilot and growth.

    The scale keeps two questions apart: how mature is the technology, and how mature is the business? A signed pilot with a big customer is good news, but the company can still be at TRL 4.

    Why the software playbook does not fit

    • Time. Software can earn revenue within months. Deep tech often takes years to its first revenue, and seven to twelve to mature, while many Indian funds run for five to seven years.
    • The moat. Software defends itself with speed and distribution. Deep tech relies on patents, know-how, scarce experts and approvals.
    • How risk falls. In software, risk falls steadily as numbers come in. In deep tech it falls in steps: the prototype works, the trial succeeds, the certificate arrives. Companies that run out of money between two steps get stuck.

    Remember

    • Know your TRL, and say it plainly in your deck.
    • Keep R&D and IP records in order from day one. They now matter for DPIIT recognition as well as for investors.
    • Plan your fundraising around technical milestones, not the calendar.

    The DPIIT notification (G.S.R. 108(E), 4 February 2026) is the authority on the deep-tech category. Other sources: NASA; IVCA Bharat DeepTech Report 2026. Views are the author’s own. Figures are as reported by the sources named, and they change quickly. This is general information, not financial advice. Always check with a legal advisor before you sign anything.
