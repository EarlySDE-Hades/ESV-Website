    Hesa: building rural commerce on trust

    How Vamsi Udayagiri and Hema Nandiraju built a phygital marketplace for Bharat, and why we invested twice.

    Rural India runs on trust. People buy from people they know, and a brand that has never been to the village is easy to ignore. Vamsi Udayagiri and Hema Nandiraju built Hesa around that reality when they founded it in Hyderabad in 2019.

    Hesa connects businesses and rural consumers “phygitally”: a digital platform, and a wide network of trained local entrepreneurs called Hesaathis, who serve households in their own communities. Through Hesa, farmers can buy inputs and sell produce, and families can reach banking, insurance and everyday products without leaving the village.

    Backing it in 2021

    In 2021 we invested in Hesa, together with a small group of co-investors. The model was harder to build than a pure app, because it needed people on the ground as well as technology. That was also what made it hard to copy.

    Growth, and a second cheque

    By 2024 Hesa had grown strongly, and our investment had returned more than five times its value. We chose to invest in the company again. For us, putting more money into a company we already know is the clearest sign of confidence we can give.

    Where Hesa is now

    Hesa is present in more than 50,000 villages, and aims to connect India’s 650,000+ villages to mainstream markets, giving businesses a reliable last-mile channel and rural households better access, choice and income. Read Hesa’s profile.

    What founders can take from it

    • Build for how your customers really behave. Hesa’s model starts from trust and relationships, not from what works in a city.
    • A hard model can be a moat. A network of people on the ground takes years to build, and that makes it difficult for others to follow.
    • Existing investors are your best next investors. Clear reporting and steady delivery are what turn a first cheque into a second one.
