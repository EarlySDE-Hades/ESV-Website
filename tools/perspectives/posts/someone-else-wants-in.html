In short

Every new round shrinks everyone’s percentage, including your ESOP pool and your first investor. But if the share price went up, what you own is worth more. An early investor can buy into the new round to keep their percentage, and when the round size is fixed, that comes out of the new investor’s share, not yours.

The house is worth more

Where Part 3 ended, the house had 150 shares: 100 for the four friends, 20 in the pool and 30 for Esha.

Now the house is worth ₹3 crore. That makes one share worth ₹3 crore ÷ 150 = ₹2,00,000, double what Esha paid. A round at a higher share price is called an up round.

A new investor, Gauri, puts in ₹60 lakh. At ₹2,00,000 a share, that buys 30 new shares. There are now 180 shares, and the house is worth ₹3.6 crore.

Everyone’s percentage falls

Before and after Gauri’s ₹60 lakh
WhoBeforeAfter
Each friend16.7%13.9%
The pool13.3%11.1%
Esha20%16.7%
Gauri0%16.7%

Everyone fell by the same proportion, because there are simply more shares now. Even the pool shrank, so Farhan’s 8 options are a smaller slice than before. A pool is not protected from the next round.

A smaller slice, worth more

Follow Aman, who has held the same 25 shares since the start.

His share of the house nearly halved, but what he owns doubled in value. That is the reason to raise: a smaller piece of something much bigger.

Esha keeps her 20%

Esha’s deal gave her pro-rata rights: the right, not a duty, to buy into later rounds and keep her percentage.

To own 20% of 180 shares, she needs 36. She has 30, so she buys 6 of the new shares for ₹12 lakh. The round is still ₹60 lakh, so Gauri puts in ₹48 lakh and gets 24 shares.

The four friends end up with the same 55.6% either way. Esha’s top-up comes out of Gauri’s share, not the founders’.

One catch: that is only true if the round stays at ₹60 lakh. If Gauri still wants all 30 shares and Esha takes her 6 as well, the round grows to ₹72 lakh, and the founders are diluted more.

Remember

  • A falling percentage is not a loss. In an up round, watch the share price, not the percentage.
  • Know which investors have pro-rata rights before you promise a new investor their share of the round.
  • Your ESOP pool shrinks too. Topping it up costs someone, and if it is done before the new money comes in, it is the founders.
