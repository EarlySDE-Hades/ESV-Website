In short

Indian deep tech raised a record $2.99 billion in 2025. But the money is uneven: very few funds back the earliest lab stage, and the number of investors falls sharply between seed and Series B. Founders need to know which gap they are in.

Record money, uneven spread

The IVCA’s Bharat DeepTech Report 2026 counts $11.4 billion of private funding across 957 deals between 2015 and mid-2026. 2025 was the best year yet, at $2.99 billion across 189 deals, even though total startup funding in India fell about 17%.

The more useful question for a founder: is money available at my stage, and who funds the round after this one?

Gap 1: the earliest science

Only 15% of the funds surveyed back companies at TRL 1 to 3, the stage of basic research. There is no product to demo and no customer to call, so the usual valuation tools struggle. The report calls this the pilot gap.

If you are here, a standard seed fund is probably not your first cheque. Expect research grants, incubators and the few funds that back lab-stage science. The government’s new RDI Scheme starts at TRL 4, so it does not fill this gap either.

Gap 2: the middle

74% of funds invest at seed, but only 46% at Series B. The average cheque nearly triples, from $6.6M to $20.9M. Only 2% of funds can write cheques above ₹100 crore, and 42% run for five to seven years against deep tech’s seven to twelve.

For an investor, that makes a high seed valuation risky: the next round has a higher bar and fewer people who can lead it.

Two policy changes

  • Twenty years, not ten. Under DPIIT’s February 2026 framework, deep-tech startups can stay recognised for up to 20 years, with a ₹300 crore turnover cap instead of ₹200 crore.
  • Patient money. The ₹1 lakh crore RDI Scheme lends through fund managers, not directly to founders. It funds TRL 4 and above.

Other countries face the same gap. Europe has plenty of startups but too little growth capital; China puts state money into the earliest stage. India’s approach sits in between, and leaves the lab stage open.

How an investor prices it

Take a made-up example. A company might be worth ₹200 crore at exit, and a fund needs 20 times its money at this stage. That points to about ₹10 crore post-money.

Remember

  • Separate technology risk from market risk, and say what the next round of money buys.
  • Keep R&D and IP records in order.
  • Show a credible path past seed: who funds your Series A and B?
  • Outside Bengaluru (51% of deep-tech deals), show how you will reach talent and investors.
