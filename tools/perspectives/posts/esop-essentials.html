In short

An ESOP pool is a set of shares kept aside to reward the people you hire. They earn their shares slowly, by staying. Watch where the pool is counted: if it comes out of the value before the investor’s money, the founders pay for all of it.

Esha wants a caretaker

Back to Part 1. Esha is about to put ₹30 lakh into the house, valued at ₹1.2 crore. This time she adds a condition:

“Set aside 20 shares for a caretaker you hire later.”

Those 20 shares are an option pool, also called an ESOP pool (employee stock option plan). It is a good idea. The question is who pays for it.

Who pays for the pool

The house is still valued at ₹1.2 crore. But that value now covers 120 shares (the friends’ 100 plus the 20 in the pool), not 100. So one share costs ₹1,00,000 instead of ₹1,20,000.

At the lower price, Esha’s ₹30 lakh buys 30 shares. She still owns exactly 20%.

Esha’s deal, without and with the pool
WhoWithout the poolWith the pool
Each friend20%, worth ₹30 lakh16.7%, worth ₹25 lakh
The poolNone13.3%, worth ₹20 lakh
Esha20%, worth ₹30 lakh20%, worth ₹30 lakh

Esha’s share did not change at all. The whole ₹20 lakh pool came from the four friends, ₹5 lakh each, for someone not yet hired.

So the friends’ 100 shares were really valued at ₹1 crore, not ₹1.2 crore. Founders call this the effective pre-money: the headline value minus the pool.

Investors asking for this is normal, not a trick. But it means the size of the pool is worth negotiating as carefully as the valuation.

Farhan joins

The friends hire Farhan and offer him 8 of the 20 pool shares. He does not get shares on day one. He gets options: the right to buy a share later at a fixed price, here ₹10. If a share is later worth ₹1,00,000, each option is worth a lot. If the house never grows, they are worth little.

Earning it over time

Farhan earns his options by staying. This is called vesting. A common plan is four years with a one-year cliff: nothing in the first year, then a quarter on his first anniversary, and the rest over the next three years.

  • Leaves after 10 months: keeps 0 options.
  • Leaves after 1 year: keeps 2.
  • Stays 4 years: keeps all 8.

Options he has not earned go back into the pool for the next hire.

Remember

  • Ask where the pool sits. If it is inside the pre-money, you pay for it.
  • Size the pool from your hiring plan up to the next round, not from a round number. Every unused share was paid for by the founders.
  • A promise of “1%” over coffee is not a grant. Options need a written scheme and approvals.
  • Always have a cliff, and explain the tax to your team. Using options can mean paying tax before anyone has sold a share.
