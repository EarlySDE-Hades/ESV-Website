In short

India spent decades fighting hunger. Now it is fighting the opposite. Packaged and ultra-processed food has spread fast, and obesity and high blood sugar are rising with it. Policy is starting to respond, and the bigger opportunity is in helping people manage their health.

Where India stands

The latest National Family Health Survey (NFHS-6), covering about 6.8 lakh households, shows the numbers that matter moving the wrong way in just four years.

The ICMR-INDIAB study estimates about 10.1 crore Indians with diabetes and 13.6 crore with prediabetes. The Economic Survey warns that obese children could rise from 3.3 crore in 2020 to 8.3 crore by 2035.

What changed on the plate

Sales of ultra-processed food in India grew more than 150% between 2009 and 2023. Three things drove it:

  • Price and reach. A ₹10 packet of biscuits or chips is cheaper than a fresh meal, and sold in every kirana store.
  • Busier lives. Working couples, long commutes and delivery apps made ready-to-eat food the default.
  • Marketing. Snacks are heavily marketed to children, often with “healthy” claims that hide the sugar and salt.

The policy response

  • The Economic Survey 2025-26 suggests the highest GST slab for ultra-processed food, and limits on its advertising.
  • FSSAI has proposed red warning labels for “High Sugar”, “High Salt” and “High Fat”, after pressure from the Supreme Court.
  • CBSE schools have put up “sugar boards” showing the sugar in common snacks.

What worked elsewhere

  • Chile combined warning labels with limits on advertising to children and bans in schools. Purchases of sugary drinks fell 23.7%.
  • Mexico taxed sugary drinks. Purchases fell 12% by the end of the first year, and 17% among low-income households.
  • The UK taxed the sugar, not the drink. Companies cut it: average sugar in soft drinks fell 47.4%.

The bottom line

Labels alone will not reverse a health crisis on this scale. The bigger opportunity is in helping Indians manage what they eat, track what it does to them, and stay on treatment for years. That is a job built for healthtech.
