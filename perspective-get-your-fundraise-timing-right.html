    Too Early or Too Late? Getting Your Fundraising Timing Right

    Revenue is not the signal to raise. The three things that are.

    Karan ShahDeal Manager, Earlyseed Ventures

    In short

    Revenue gets you into the room, but timing decides what you walk out with. In 2026, seed money in India went to fewer founders. Raise when three signals line up: proof that repeats, a clear use of the money, and runway to walk away.

    The market has changed

    Investors are writing fewer, bigger cheques to companies that have already proved something. Tracxn’s figures for January to September 2026:

    The road past seed is getting longer too: the median gap from seed to Series A in India is now about 20 months.

    Too early

    The first few lakhs of revenue feel like proof, but often come from the founder’s network, a paid pilot or one big customer who signed once. Raising on thin proof means a lower valuation, investors who are hard to win back, and a next round judged on what you promised in this one.

    Too late

    The business is doing well, the raise keeps getting pushed, and then there are four months of cash left. A seed round takes about 170 days to close, and only 1 in 7 startups that start a raise close one. Investors can tell when you need the money more than they need the deal, and the terms get worse.

    The three signals

    Raise when all three line up. Any two without the third, and you are either early or late.

    One more rule: raise right after a milestone, not right before it. Close the big customer first, then raise on the result.

    When not to raise

    • You are profitable and growing on your own cash.
    • You cannot name what the money unlocks.
    • Your numbers have not moved since your last round.
    • A grant, revenue-based financing or a working capital line fits the need better.
